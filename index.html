      Methodology & sourcesHow to read these numbers
      • Peak, not throughput. Every figure is a theoretical or advertised ceiling. Real transfers are lower.
      • One direction. Full-duplex links are never summed. Asymmetric modes show the faster direction and state the reverse rate.
      • Raw signaling or encoding-adjusted. The Figures switch applies to every number on the page. Encoding-adjusted (default) subtracts a documented line code (8b/10b, 128b/130b) from PCIe 1.0–5.0, SATA, SD UHS-II/III and the connectors that carry them; raw signaling shows their line rate. It is still not usable throughput: packet and protocol overhead remain. Memory is the same in both. Rows with no recorded line-code factor (PCIe 6.0+, SAS, DisplayPort, HDMI, USB, Thunderbolt, Ethernet) stay as listed, and their details say so.
      • Decimal units. 1 GB/s = 109 bytes/s. 1 Gb/s = 109 bits/s. GB/s = Gb/s ÷ 8.
      • Memory. Data rate per pin × bus width ÷ 8. DDR and LPDDR use a 64-bit channel (LPDDR normalized), except LPDDR6 at 24 bits. HBM is per stack.
      • Connectors are not speeds. U.2, U.3 and M.2 are form factors and NVMe is a protocol; their rows repeat the underlying PCIe or SATA link. Ethernet transceiver form factors (SFP, QSFP, OSFP…) are shapes, not rates, and have their own guide. Only the MSA-verified OSFP and OSFP-XD module capacities are in this catalog, one row per OSFP MSA rate/lane mapping, marked Connector / form factor: they are aggregate module capacity, not usable payload, and not an IEEE Ethernet PHY status (see the Ethernet rows for that). The other guide rows remain out of the catalog.
